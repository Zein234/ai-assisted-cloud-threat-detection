## Title: Multiple Failed Login Attempts

### Incident Overview
Detects when there are 5 or more failed login attempts into a windows machine from the same source IP address within 10 minutes

Case assigned to: Zein Abdulkarim

#### Attack Information:

* Ip: SOURCE_IP_FROM_INCIDENT
* LogInAttempts: 6
* TargetAccounts: azureuser
* LogonTypes: 7 - Unlock
* FaliureCodes: 0xc000006d / 0xc000006a

#### AI-Assisted Analysis
1. **Incident Assessment**  
This incident involves multiple failed login attempts to a Windows machine (`vm-windows-dete`) from IP address `SOURCE_IP_FROM_INCIDENT`, with a total of 6 failed login attempts (as indicated in `custom_details.LogInAttempts`). The detection rule triggered due to 5 or more failed attempts from the same source within 10 minutes. However, the timeline shows a successful login (Event ID 4624) from the same IP (`SOURCE_IP_FROM_INCIDENT`) shortly after the failed attempts, suggesting a potential credential brute-force attack that succeeded. The presence of multiple `4625` (failed login) and `4624` (successful login) events, along with `4798` (group enumeration), `4648` (explicit credential logon), and `4673` (privileged service calls), indicates a possible attempt to escalate privileges or perform reconnaissance post-compromise.

Despite the detection of failed attempts, **a successful compromise is not confirmed**—the successful login (4624) is followed by privileged operations (4673, 4672), which may indicate post-exploitation activity. However, without direct evidence of unauthorized access, data exfiltration, or persistence, the attack is not confirmed as successful.

2. **Important Timeline Findings**  
- At `18:27:40`, a successful login (4624) occurs for `azureuser` from `SOURCE_IP_FROM_INCIDENT`, immediately followed by a series of privileged operations (4673, 4672, 4688).  
- Multiple `4625` (failed logons) occur between `18:27:40` and `18:28:05`, indicating a brute-force attempt.  
- A successful login (4624) occurs again at `18:29:14` from the same IP, with subsequent privileged operations and group enumeration (4798).  
- The `4648` event (explicit credential logon) and `4798` (group enumeration) suggest the attacker may have obtained valid credentials and performed reconnaissance.  
- The `4801` (workstation unlocked) and `4800` (workstation locked) events indicate session manipulation.  
- The pattern of failed attempts followed by a successful login and subsequent privileged actions suggests a **credential brute-force attack that succeeded**, possibly leading to further exploitation.

3. **Recommended Investigation Steps**  
- **Verify the identity of the `azureuser` account**: Check if this account has elevated privileges or access to sensitive systems.  
- **Investigate the session after the successful login**: Review process creation (4688), scheduled tasks (4702), and registry changes to identify suspicious activity.  
- **Check for lateral movement**: Look for `4673` events (privileged service calls) and `4688` (new process creation) outside of normal user behavior.  
- **Examine network traffic**: Look for outbound connections from `vm-windows-dete` during the time window to detect data exfiltration or C2 communication.

#### Investigation Performed
* Reviewed the authentication activity associated with source IP `SOURCE_IP_FROM_INCIDENT` and the `azureuser` account.
* The surrounding timeline was reviewed to determine whether the failed authentication attempts were followed by successful access or additional suspicious activity. 
* Successful logon events and subsequent Windows security events were present in the surrounding telemetry. However, these events occurred during the controlled lab simulation and were generated by expected activity on the test environment.
* The source IP, target account, and timing of the events were consistent with the activity intentionally generated to test the detection rule. No evidence of an unauthorized user, unexpected persistence, lateral movement, or other genuine compromise was identified during the investigation.

### Conclusion
The alert was a true detection of repeated authentication failures from the simulated source IP. The activity was intentionally generated as part of a controlled lab simulation to validate the Windows failed-login detection rule.
Although successful authentication and additional Windows security events were present in the surrounding timeline, manual investigation confirmed that these were associated with expected lab activity rather than an unauthorized compromise.
This incident was closed as Benign Positive – Suspicious but Expected.
